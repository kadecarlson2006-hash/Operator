| 43 | The decision model has never judged a real conversation | PARTIALLY VERIFIED | Two drill runs, 2026-09-06, `openai/gpt-5.6-luna`. **0 of 4** ambient scenarios spoke both times, always by the model's own choice (`suppressedAfterModel` false everywhere). The grounded experiment is the strongest evidence: the disputed invoice conversation was silent for want of knowledge, and **spoke once the terms were in memory** - "I have Halvorsen Supply's payment terms recorded as net thirty", confidence 0.95, relevance 0.9, labelling the memory *as* memory rather than asserting it as live fact. | 44 | Decision thresholds are estimates | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY (now measurable) | Still untested: the floors judge only a model that wants to speak, and across two drill runs it never did. Milestone 14 adds the instrument rather than the answer - `GET /feedback/summary` reports the mean confidence and relevance of the comments the user actually rejected, which is the first evidence of what the floors should have been. It needs real verdicts from real use; nothing is measured yet. |
| 45 | Ambient deciding is not yet automatic | ACCEPTED (v1) | Nothing polls the transcript and asks on its own; a decision happens when the user presses a button. Wiring it to fire on new speech is Milestone 13, and doing it before the thresholds are tuned would risk exactly the nuisance the policy exists to prevent. |
| 46 | Repetition matching is lexical | ACCEPTED (v1) | "Already said" compares word overlap, so the same point made in genuinely different words will slip through. An embedding comparison would catch it and costs a call; revisit if it proves to be a real annoyance. |
| 47 | The settle delay before an ambient decision is a guess | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY | 2.5 s was chosen as "a pause in speech rather than a gap between words" and never measured. Too short and Operator interrupts; too long and it answers a question the room has moved past. **Risk 7 now measures 2 s of model latency on top of it**, so the room waits about 4.5 s before Operator speaks, before synthesis. The delay may need to come *down* to compensate rather than being tuned in isolation. Measure the whole path in Milestone 13 before changing either number. |
| 48 | The decision budget defaults are estimates | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY | 20 s between decisions and 12 per five minutes bound the spend, but nobody has measured what an hour of real conversation actually costs, or whether that budget is too mean to be useful. `GET /usage` reports the answer once a real key exists. |
| 49 | Active Operator has never run against a real model | PARTIALLY VERIFIED | The decision half ran for real on 2026-09-06 (risk 43) and stayed silent. The rest of the loop - lull detection, speaking, joining the transcript - is still fakes only, and needs the phone. Ambient decision latency measured at 1447-1981 ms, faster than the 2019-3782 ms seen on `/ai/respond`, which softens the compounding worry in risk 47 without removing it. |
| 50 | A declined decision discards the model's own confidence and relevance | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY | `ModelDecisionEngine.parse` turns any `shouldSpeak: false` into `ResponseDecision.silence(...)`, which hardcodes confidence 1.0 and relevance 0.0; only the reason code survives. Those columns are therefore placeholders on every silent row, not measurements. It costs nothing today because the floors are not consulted when the model declines - but it destroys the one diagnostic needed to tune them, since "how close did it come to speaking" is exactly what is thrown away. Preserving the reported scores on a silent decision would make risk 44 measurable. |
| 51 | Decision reason codes are model-authored free text, not a fixed vocabulary | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY | The same scenarios produced different codes across two runs minutes apart: the invoice case gave NO_VERIFIED_INFORMATION then NO_CONFIRMED_FACT, and the unanswered question gave NOTHING_WORTH_SAYING then NO_DIRECT_REQUEST. `parse` takes whatever string the model supplies. They are readable diagnostics but **not stable identifiers**, so counting or comparing them across runs will not work, and the app must not branch on them. Only the codes from `ConversationPolicy` (MUTED, RECENTLY_SPOKE, DECIDED_RECENTLY and the rest) are fixed. |
| 52 | COMMENT NOW can decline, and does | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY | The same COMMENT_NOW request spoke in one run (LIGHT_RELEVANT_COMMENT) and returned NOTHING_WORTH_SAYING in the next. That is permitted by design - the prompt says the user wants to hear from you *if you have anything worth saying* - but it means a button press can visibly do nothing, which is a user-facing question rather than a correctness one. Decide whether COMMENT NOW should be allowed to decline silently, acknowledge, or always answer. |
| 53 | The feedback penalty numbers are estimates, like everything else in the decision path | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY | 0.05 per complaint, capped at 0.25, decaying over an hour (ADR-045) were chosen to be explainable and to be a nudge rather than a mute. Nobody has given Operator real feedback yet, so it is unknown whether one complaint changes behaviour noticeably enough to feel responsive, or so much that the user feels they have broken it. Tune against `GET /feedback/summary` once real verdicts exist. |
| 54 | The remote only works while Operator is in the foreground | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY | Key events reach `RemoteControlCoordinator` through `MainActivity`, so a button pressed with the screen off or another app in front does nothing - which is most of the time a remote is worth having. Fixing it means a MediaSession that survives the screen going off. Worth building once hardware exists to test it. **Check first whether the Ray-Ban glasses' own capacitive surface reaches an app as a media key**: if it does, Milestone 15 is testable with no extra hardware, and the answer also feeds risk 2 (Meta AI coexistence). |
| 55 | The remote gesture timings are estimates | UNKNOWN TO VERIFY | 500 ms for a hold and a 350 ms double-press window were chosen to be distinguishable, not measured on a real button. The window is the more awkward of the two: every single tap is delayed by it, so it is as short as a deliberate double tap allows rather than as long as one might comfortably take. Tune on hardware. |
